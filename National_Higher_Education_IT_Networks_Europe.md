# National Higher Education IT Networks and Collaborative Structures in Europe

Research and retrieval date: **7 October 2026**. Result: **85 organisations or independently named collaborative structures in 44 of the 50 countries/search areas examined**. This is a systematic inventory, not an official or exhaustive register.

## Scope of the search

The [15 national EUNIS partners](https://eunis.org/partners/) suggest three types: national professional and leadership associations for IT (for example ZKI and UCISA); shared IT service providers with a higher education remit (for example SURF, CSC and OPI); and long-term software and systems consortia (for example Ladok, MUCI and SIGMA). Accordingly, the inventory covers not only formal membership associations, but also long-term national collaborative structures and jointly commissioned institutions.

Included: an explicit higher education remit; IT co-ordination, collaborative IT development or IT operations as a core task; a nationwide remit or cross-regional sectoral collaboration. National IT leadership forums may be organisationally located within higher education associations or network providers. Excluded: individual higher education IT departments without a national remit; purely regional consortia; commercial providers without collective higher education ownership or governance; event series only; international umbrella associations; consortia solely concerned with media licensing or library procurement; and general computer science professional societies.

## Country framework and search approach

The starting point is the 44 sovereign states in the UN M49 Europe region, excluding dependent territories: [UN M49](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/).

Albania; Andorra; Austria; Belarus; Belgium; Bosnia and Herzegovina; Bulgaria; Croatia; Czechia; Denmark; Estonia; Finland; France; Germany; Greece; Hungary; Iceland; Ireland; Italy; Latvia; Liechtenstein; Lithuania; Luxembourg; Malta; Monaco; Montenegro; Netherlands; North Macedonia; Norway; Poland; Portugal; Republic of Moldova; Romania; Russia; San Marino; Serbia; Slovakia; Slovenia; Spain; Sweden; Switzerland; Ukraine; United Kingdom; Vatican City / Holy See.

Separately listed extension: Armenia; Azerbaijan; Cyprus; Georgia; Kosovo; Türkiye. Armenia, Azerbaijan, Cyprus, Georgia and Türkiye are members of the [Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/about-us/our-member-states), but are statistically assigned to Asia by UN M49; Kosovo is treated as a separate search area. These six cases appear only in Section C and can be removed if a narrower geographical definition of Europe is required.

For each country: searches combined higher education terms with *IT association*, *CIO network*, *shared services*, *consortium*, *university information systems* and *national research and education network*; additional searches were conducted in the relevant national languages; organisational websites were checked; and collaborations mentioned there were followed up. EUNIS served as the initial set, while the [GÉANT membership list](https://geant.org/about-geant/membership/) was used as an additional check for national infrastructure providers. Results outside the country framework were not included.

## Reading key

- **★** marks the 15 original examples from EUNIS. EN denotes an English-language profile page; other language codes indicate the available language version.
- Membership figures refer to the category stated in each case. Member institutions, individual members, owners and founders are not treated as equivalent. Customer numbers, network connections, campus locations and users are not counted as members.
- `membercount undefined` means that no robust figure could be derived from the sources reviewed, or that no suitable membership model exists. Counts of listed entries are our own counts of the linked directories; not all directories have an unambiguous update date. The figures therefore do not constitute a uniform survey as at the research date.

## Coverage, boundaries and remaining uncertainties

For **Andorra, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City / the Holy See**, no current and sufficiently well-evidenced national organisation meeting the criteria used could be verified. This is a research finding, not evidence that no such organisation exists. Liechtenstein uses infrastructure provided by Switch, among others. SARNET was not counted as a Bosnia-wide organisation because its remit relates specifically to Republika Srpska.

The total refers to collaborative structures, not exclusively to legally independent entities. For example, the Czech IT leadership forum is hosted within CESNET. Services and predecessor organisations are not counted as additional networks or consortia: Funet within CSC, Janet within Jisc, RCTS within FCCN, HEAnet/EduCampus within Asiera, Uninett/Unit/NSD within Sikt, and the Hungarian network infrastructure now operated by Pro-M.

The boundary between a broad shared IT service provider and a research network operator is fluid. Section B is therefore an extension, not a claim that all operators listed there are professional associations comparable to ZKI or UCISA. Purely specialised research, computing and library consortia were not systematically explored as a separate search universe.
