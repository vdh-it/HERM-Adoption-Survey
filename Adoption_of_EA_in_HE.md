# Adoption of Enterprise Architecture in Higher Education

When asked why Enterprise Architecture (EA) matters, most CIOs would answer by referring to its enhanced ability to steer, govern and plan changes to an organisation’s digital support structures and services.

While this may sound generic, the EA community in Higher Education (HE) is highly diverse. Institutions differ significantly in terms of their EA maturity, the reasons they have for introducing EA, and the challenges they face. Some institutions still lack transparency and consistent information about their digital infrastructures and services, and a shared overview of these. Others have a good understanding of these areas but struggle to align business needs with IT capabilities closely enough.

The EUNIS EA Special Interest Group (SIG) aims to support its members and institutions in adopting and further developing EA. To achieve this effectively, we would like to collect practical insights from the community for the community, such as success stories, challenges, shortcomings, use cases and reasons why EA is — or is not — adopted in HE institutions.

Such a survey would create value on several levels. Firstly, it would raise the profile of EA as a key issue in the digital transformation of HE. It could support institutions that are currently considering introducing EA, creating positive momentum by demonstrating that other institutions are already addressing similar issues. It would also raise the profile of EUNIS as a community that actively supports strategic digital transformation.

Additionally, the survey is aligned with the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with our international partner communities at CAUDIT, EDUCAUSE, and UCISA. It could help transform existing collaborative ambitions into concrete joint activities. The survey could also help to identify target groups for more detailed follow-up surveys, interviews or workshops, taking a human-centred approach to understanding the needs of different institutions and roles.

## Planned survey

The EA SIG therefore plans to conduct a survey. The online questionnaire will be short and precise (5-10 minutes), but open enough to provide an initial overview of the current situation. The intention is to start small and build on the initial results by conducting more in-depth research later.

The survey will collect practical and relevant information on where EA is currently applied, why it is used, the scenarios it supports and the challenges institutions face. It should also provide insight into the adoption of EA frameworks, including HERM, and the reasons why institutions decide against introducing EA or have not yet started. An initial draft of the questionnaire was suggested and discussed by the EA SIG during the pre-conference day in Timișoara.

The results of the survey are intended to be owned by and developed for the community. We aim for shared community ownership under the same licence model as the Higher Education Reference Model (HERM): CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. This licence model supports the reuse and further development of the results, while ensuring attribution, non-commercial use and sharing under the same licence conditions.

Any external support would be responsible for conducting, analysing, and documenting the work, not taking ownership of the results. The results should generate shared knowledge to help institutions understand their own EA status and coverage profile as well as the associated challenges and opportunities in relation to others.

The EA SIG discussed the option of performing such a survey one year ago. At that time, however, the necessary time, resources and personal engagement were unavailable. Currently, we can take some initial steps by sharing the workload collectively. However, this will take several months, and it is unclear how much can realistically be achieved through voluntary community work alone.

The EA SIG is therefore asking the EUNIS Board of Directors for support in two ways.

## 1. Support for communication and participation

Firstly, the EA SIG would like the Board's full support when sending the call for participation to national bodies in countries where EUNIS has active members. This could increase EUNIS's visibility among national bodies.

Initially, we do not plan to distribute the survey primarily through the EUNIS email list. We believe that the survey would receive more attention if national collaboration bodies addressed their communities directly. These bodies often have a broader overview of their respective HE landscapes and can explain the survey's relevance from their own perspective.

As the expected results will be relevant not only to EUNIS but also to national bodies in their own work, we suggest that each national distribution hub answers the 'why' question from its own perspective. The EA SIG will provide templates and support to help them address their members, as this will be crucial for achieving a high response rate.

Following the initial rollout through the national bodies, EUNIS could use its established email distribution channels to send reminders before the survey closes. This would help to increase participation and provide a more comprehensive overview of the HE landscape.

## Governance, publication, and data handling

The survey will be conducted in full compliance with GDPR requirements. The questionnaire will clearly state the survey's purpose, how the data will be used and published, and how contact details will be handled.

The results will first be discussed within the EA SIG. Following this internal discussion, the results will be published by the EA SIG together with the contributors who were actively involved in the analysis and reporting.

The publication package will include the questionnaire, the analysis and the cleaned dataset. Where necessary, data will be published in anonymised or aggregated form. Contact email addresses will not be published and will only be used for agreed follow-up activities related to further EA work. Respondents will also be asked whether their institution may be named in the published dataset, or if their responses should only be used in an pseudoanonymised form. 

Where appropriate, the data and outputs should be openly available on Zenodo or a comparable repository under an agreed community licence. The exact publication format will depend on the consent given by respondents and the anonymisation requirements identified when preparing the survey.

## Timeline and deliverables

Ideally, the survey should be ready by the end of summer so that it can be conducted during the winter term, preferably in September and October.

The expected deliverables (D) are as follows:
1. the final survey questionnaire;
2. communication templates for national bodies and EUNIS channels;
3. raw data for internal processing, subject to GDPR requirements;
4. a cleaned dataset for publication in either pseudonymised or open form, depending on consent and agreed publication rules;
5. an initial analysis report for the EA SIG and the EUNIS Board;
6. a report or paper for the community;
7. follow-up activities, including more detailed surveys, interviews or workshops.

The minimum viable survey (D1–D5) provides core value to the inner EA community. Depending on the results and discussions additional publications can extend the visibility of our results (D6). After completing these initial steps, the EA SIG will either broaden the scope of the work or revise critical parts of the survey before engaging with the wider global community (D7). This would bring the initiative into line with the ongoing MoU-based collaboration between the EA communities, including future partners such as AXIES in Japan. This approach could potentially be transferred to other EUNIS SIGs, building the value of data-driven knowledge.
