# EA and HERM Adoption Survey — Analysis Matrix

**Purpose:** Define in advance which research questions can be answered from which variables, at which unit of analysis, and with which type of analysis.

**Aligned with:** Blueprint, last updated 2026-10-04. All question numbers refer to this version.

---

## C. Planned Descriptive Analyses

### C1. Sample profile

- Number of respondents
- Number of identifiable institutions
- Number of countries represented
- Institution type distribution
- Institution size distribution
- Respondent role distribution
- Respondent organisational unit distribution
- Respondent knowledge-of-EA distribution

### C2. EA landscape

- EA status distribution (most advanced part of the institution)
- EA coverage across organisational areas: per area, coverage type, central–academic gap
- Age of operational EA practice
- Approaches used, grouped into:
  - EA frameworks / methods
  - higher-education reference models
  - modelling languages
  - institution-specific approaches
- Organisational home
- EA leadership model

### C3. HERM landscape

Report at least three separate indicators:

1. **HERM adoption (headline, Blueprint OI-5):** pilot + operational + embedded
2. **HERM operational adoption:** active operational use + embedded in governance
3. **HERM engagement:** evaluating + pilot + operational + embedded

Also report:

- discontinued HERM use
- respondent familiarity with HERM separately
- artefact use by stage and additional artefacts
- application areas
- tasks/use cases
- reported value
- enabling factors
- integration with broader EA
- adaptations, extensions and mappings

### C4. HERM barriers and future adoption

- Frequency of each barrier
- Primary barrier distribution
- Future consideration of HERM
- Potential role of HERM
- Support needs and highest-priority support need

### C5. Qualitative outputs

Apply a documented coding scheme to:

- 4.5 primary application area (capability names / identifiers)
- 4.8 concrete use cases
- 4.9 what worked well
- 4.11 difficulties / gaps
- 5.5 adoption requirements
- 6.1 open EA questions
- 6.2 open HERM questions

For each coding exercise retain:

- raw response
- assigned code(s)
- coder
- coding version
- optional memo / interpretation note

## E. Analyses That Require Additional Design Decisions

### E1. Country comparisons

Do not treat country differences as population differences unless recruitment coverage and minimum country-level sample sizes are adequate. Countries with few institutions or responses may be pooled into a combined group for group-wise comparison (Blueprint OI-10); define the minimum group size and the pooling rule before analysis.

### E2. Institution-level prevalence estimates

Do not report estimates for European higher education institutions as a population unless the sampling frame supports this claim.

### E3. Multiple respondents from one institution

Proposed handling (see Blueprint OI-2):

1. Primary analysis at respondent level: retain all respondents as independent expert observations.
2. Institution-level counts (e.g. number of institutions with an operational EA practice): select one response per institution by a predefined rule — highest knowledge in 1.8; if tied, the most complete response.
3. Report institution-level results as a sensitivity analysis alongside respondent-level results.
4. Do not reconcile or merge responses. Use the respondent's organisational unit (1.7) to interpret differing answers, especially in 2.1 and 2.2.

### E4. EA status and coverage profile instead of EA maturity

The Blueprint does not measure a validated multi-dimensional EA maturity construct (Blueprint OI-11). It does capture several dimensions that can be reported together as an **EA status and coverage profile**:

- highest EA status in the institution (2.1)
- coverage across organisational areas (2.2)
- age of the operational EA practice (2.3)
- organisational anchoring and lead (2.5, 2.6)
- HERM status and integration (3.2, 4.12)

Report these as a profile and avoid the term "EA maturity". Do not combine them into a single maturity score.

### E5. Framework-specific comparison

The current Blueprint records which approaches are used (2.4), but not separate duration/status/purpose for every selected framework. Detailed framework-by-framework comparison requires a repeated block.

### E6. Transferability of cases

Do not infer transferability solely from respondent self-assessment. Use the criteria decided in Blueprint OI-8 (see `CASE_CANDIDATE`).

### E7. Peer matching

Peer matching is an operational service for the community, not a research output.

- Use only respondents who selected peer matching in 7.2 and provided an e-mail address in 8.1.
- Match only between respondents who both selected peer matching (mutual opt-in).
- Build matches from `PEER_MATCH_PROFILE`; share contact details only with the matched peer (see wording in 8.1).
- Keep the matching list separate from the research dataset (Blueprint data rule 12).

## F. Minimum Data-Quality Checks

Before substantive analysis:

0. Exclude responses with 0.1 = Preview or Leave (Blueprint data rule 13).
1. Check duplicate institution names and likely spelling variants.
2. Check mutually exclusive selections (`None`, `Don't know`, substantive responses).
3. Check routing consistency, in particular no value in 2.3 for respondents with 2.1 = exploring, and no values in 2.2–2.6 for respondents with 2.1 = no EA practice or don't know.
4. Do not treat a HERM engagement (4.1) longer than the EA practice age (2.3) as an error; HERM may precede an operational EA practice.
5. Check HERM status against HERM artefact/task responses.
6. Check EA status against EA approach responses.
7. Check 2.1 against 2.2: the most advanced coverage row (lowest substantive code) should equal 2.1 (`EA_COVERAGE_CONSISTENT`); flag deviations.
8. Flag low respondent knowledge (1.8) for sensitivity analyses.
9. Distinguish item non-response from explicit `Don't know` responses.
10. Preserve raw multi-select variables; do not collapse them prematurely.
11. Flag peer-matching selection (7.2) without an e-mail address (8.1).
12. Document all recoding and derived variables in a reproducible codebook.

## G. Suggested Reporting Language

Unless the sampling design is strengthened, prefer formulations such as:

- "Among participating institutions..."
- "Among respondents who reported an operational EA practice..."
- "X institutions reported an operational EA practice in at least one part of the institution..."
- "Among institutions with an operational EA practice, EA covered faculties in X cases..."
- "HERM was reported as being in operational use by..."
- "The most frequently reported barriers were..."
- "Respondents most frequently identified the following support needs..."

Avoid without an appropriate sampling basis:

- "X% of European universities..."
- "HERM adoption in Europe is X%..."
- country rankings interpreted as population differences.

Avoid in any case:

- "X% have established EA" without noting that 2.1 records the most advanced part of the institution;
- "EA maturity", maturity scores, or maturity rankings (see E4).
